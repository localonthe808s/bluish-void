### Finding (medium): bound noon locks are overconfident; 11-14 was bound at 10:55 AM

- **What the trail shows.**
  - At 10:55 the 10:51 hourly reading of 44.06 became the floor, 44.822 after the offset. That met or beat the forecast centre, so `binding_now` (`kalshi_daily.py:4641`, `obs_far >= fadj`) went true.
  - sd fell from 0.841 to 0.675, and p on "45 or below" rose from 0.70 to 0.837. The sheet showed the day as `over`.
  - The sheet's own peak-behind-us figure at that hour was **p 0.158** (`peak_p` in the trail). Its archive said the high was most likely still ahead.
  - 83 rows held p > 0.6 on a losing bracket. The run went from 00:30 to 15:50 and stayed at 0.77-0.83 after the 12:06 lock.
  - The flip to 46-47 came at 15:55. The CLI's 46 at 3:40 PM was posted by 16:45, and `decided` followed at 20:55.
- **The pattern across live locks** (derived from `kalshi_ny.json` history, where bound means pred = obs_at_lock):

  | locks | n | said | hit | market hit |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | bound | 15 | 0.836 | 10/15 (0.67) | 13/15 |
  | unbound | 57 | 0.645 | 41/57 (0.72) | 41/57 |

  - The bound locks claim 0.84 and deliver 0.67.
  - Both recent bound misses are high days that rose after noon: 11-11 (-1.25) and 11-14 (-1.18).
- **Why no fix.** The binding rule ignores `peak_done`. Gating it on that figure, or not collapsing sd before the archive says the peak is likely done, would be a modelling change with its own replay. That is for the owner, not a surgical fix. n = 15 is also small.

### Finding (medium): the low is never "decided", even with the climate report in hand

- At 23:01 the ny_low row had `cli` 38.0, `six` 37.94, and a current reading of 43 (21:51), rising to 42 by 22:51. Yet the sheet's p on the winning 38-39 bracket was only 0.561, and 0.44 sat on 36-37. The market had 0.99.
- From 12:06 on, the pick flipped between 38-39 and 36-37 seven times. Each flip to 36-37 (p 0.56-0.64) was a forecast-dropout row. One of them came at 23:04, with p 0.641 on the loser.
- **Cause, as far as the code shows.**
  - `kalshi_lows.py:697` writes `'day_decided': False` unconditionally.
  - The CLI minimum is used only as a ceiling (`cap`, line 664). The hourly-minus-offset estimate (37.55-37.6) sits below it and keeps mass on 37 and colder.
  - The highs have `decided_by_exact`. The lows have no counterpart.
- **No fix.** Adding a decision rule is a new feature. 11-13 also showed that a preliminary exact figure can be wrong by five degrees, so the rule needs its own evidence. The 08:01 lock picked the winner, so the record is unaffected.

### Finding (medium): the bake stalled 8 times in 24 h (1 the night before)

- **`kalshi-nyc.yml`, 292 runs from 11-14 10:00Z to 11-15 10:05Z:** 251 succeeded, 40 were cancelled and 1 was in progress.
- **The shape is the same every time.** One run hits the job's 25-minute `timeout-minutes` and is cancelled. The four queued behind it are each cancelled after 5 minutes.
- **The stalled runs:**

  | run | started (Z) |
  |---|---|
  | 16762 | 11-14 16:05 |
  | 16769 | 11-14 16:40 |
  | 16785 | 11-14 18:00 |
  | 16826 | 11-14 21:20 |
  | 16849 | 11-14 23:15 |
  | 16936 | 11-15 06:25 |
  | 16967 | 11-15 09:00 |
  | 16973 | 11-15 09:30 |

  - Each stall shows in both trails as a gap of about 30 minutes: 04:16, 11:30, 12:06, 13:26, 16:45 and 18:40 ET on 11-14.
  - Run 16769 left no bake between 11:38 and 12:06, so the **noon lock was taken at 12:06**.
- **Where it hangs.** Job log of run 43697127941 (16967): the step "Fetch market, forecast and observations" printed `ny_high tuned: {...}` at 09:00:53Z (`kalshi_daily.py:5949`) and nothing more until it was cancelled at 09:25:51Z. After that line, the per-market work runs in threads that buffer their log (`_TL.log`). A hung market therefore leaves no trace of which fetch it was stuck in.
- Every `urlopen` in `kalshi_daily.py` has a timeout. That timeout is per socket operation, though, not a total, so a host that trickles bytes can still hold a thread past the job limit. This is a hypothesis, not established.
- **No fix.** The cause is not pinned down. A first step for the owner: flush `_TL.log` on a watchdog, or put a wall-clock budget on `run_market`, so the next stall names its host.
