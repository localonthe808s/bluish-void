## Findings

### 1. High: a timed-out live forecast makes the sheet call the overnight reading at 100% (fixed on this branch)

Evidence (trail `ny_high_2026-11-17.jsonl`, 59 rows, 00:00 to 05:00 ET):
- 16 rows have `models: null`. Every one of them has `pred 44.81`, which equals `obs.hmax` (44.813, the 00:55 ET
  reading), with `p 1.0` on "54 or below". The market had 57-58 at 0.585 to 0.635 throughout.
- The other 43 rows have six models and `pred` 55.9 to 56.6.
- These rows alternate with good ones: 01:20, 01:30, 01:40, 02:01, 02:10, 02:31, 02:40, 02:50, 02:56, 03:20, 03:31,
  03:51, 04:20, 04:30, 04:50 and 05:00. That gives 29 pick flips before 5 AM.
- Run 44060791125 (10:00Z = the 05:00 ET row) logs `live run unavailable (<urlopen error _ssl.c:983: The handshake
  operation timed out>); today falls back to archive`, with `open-meteo.com 125s/2 +2retry`. The run is green.
- The same pattern appears on earlier days. Model-less rows with `p > 0.9`: 15 on 11-12, 20 on 11-13, 27 on 11-14,
  1 on 11-15, 10 on 11-16 and 16 on 11-17. Before noon on 11-14 there were 21, with "45 or below" at 0.93 against
  the market's 46-47. The 11-14 lock missed, but its run had models, so this bug did not cause that miss.

Cause: `forecast_runs` (`kalshi_daily.py`, around line 1945) takes today's hours **only** from the live run, because
the archive's `end_date` is yesterday. "Falls back to archive" therefore means that no model has any hour of today.
`point_forecast` then returns None, and `run_market` sets `pred = max(obs_far, None)`, which is the floor alone.
The day is not over and not binding, so the full forecast spread stays. The ladder then puts everything on the
bracket that holds the overnight reading. `snapshot()`, which builds the noon lock, has the same `max(fl, pf)`
fallback. A lock written by such a run would stand for the day.

Fix (this branch, `_kalshi/kalshi_daily.py`, next to the existing `no forecast and no observations yet` exit): when no
model has any hour of today, print a line and `return 0`. This is the same exit the bake already uses, so `main()`
folds no digest and the last good bake stays on the sheet. The next run, 5 minutes later, retries, and the noon lock
is made by the first run at or after noon that has a forecast.

Before/after: an offline replay of `forecast_runs` for ny_high (6 models). The archive is stubbed with a 40-day
response shaped like Open-Meteo's. The live call either answers or raises the logged handshake timeout. The floor is
44.813, as in the trail.

| live call | models with an hour today | `point_forecast` | `pred` on main | new guard |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| answers | 6 of 6 | 54.6 | 54.6 | does not fire |
| times out | 0 of 6 | None | **44.813** (the floor) | fires: not written, last bake stands |

The live bake was not run here, because it needs the exchange. After the merge, the first timed-out run should print
`ny_high: no forecast hour for today (live run unavailable) -- not writing, the last bake stands`, and the trail
should stop showing `models: null` rows.

Known limits of the fix:
- If Open-Meteo stays unreachable for hours, the sheet holds its last good bake, and the noon lock waits for the first
  run with a forecast. That is the same blind spot as last night's finding 2: a `return 0` is invisible to the
  watchdog.
- `rescore.py` calls `run_market` too. A rescore that meets a live-call outage now stops with no scratch file instead
  of scoring today on the floor. Rerunning it fixes that.
- The lows (`kalshi_lows.py`) log the same timeout. They had only 2 null-pred rows tonight, and their market pull
  (1.0) masks the problem. They were not changed.
