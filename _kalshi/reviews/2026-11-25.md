At 00:56 the runs' rest-of-day lows were HRRR 33.1, GFS 33.1, ICON 35.5, ECMWF 35.8, GEM 35.9 and NBM 36.5. All
six are under the 37.94 in hand. So `bind = fl >= pf` was false, and the hour was made binding by the rule that
follows it (`snapshot`, `_kalshi/kalshi_lows.py:488-497`). That rule fires when the hour is before
`BIND_WHEN_BEFORE` (14) and the median run puts the rest-of-day low before `BIND_EVE_FROM` (18). At 1 AM the runs
put the low at dawn. So the rule set the centre to the reading, cut sd from 1.95 to 0.657 (`K.OFFSET_SD`), and let
the gap table speak.

The rule's comment gives two reasons for it: the runs' "low" before the evening is "only the next hour or two of
the same morning", and the rule applies "when the morning is what is in hand". Its evidence is the 28 live 8 AM
locks plus an archive replay. At 1 AM neither reason holds, because the whole night is still ahead. The rule has
no lower bound on the hour.

The rule has done this before. 85 to 90 of the 96 pre-8 AM rows were binding on each of 11-19, 11-20, 11-21 and
11-22. On the first three days it put 1.0 on an open "or above" bracket at about 00:55, from a reading 7 F above the
low that settled (11-19: reading 53.06, settled 46). Those brackets hit only because they were open-ended. 11-25 is
the first day it has put more than 0.6 on a closed bracket that the reading then ruled out. The 8 AM lock, which is
what is scored, is inside the rule's tested range, and the sheet is right now.

No fix. Bounding the rule to the hours it was tested on (for example `hour >= LOCK_HOUR`) changes a learned rule.
That needs a replay of the overnight hours, and it is the owner's call.
